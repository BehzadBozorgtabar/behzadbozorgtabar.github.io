STORM: Soft Tactile Memory for Safe Physical-AI Correction of Robotic Foundation Models

STORM will investigate how robots can use touch to detect unexpected physical changes and safely adjust their actions. By combining soft, fluid-filled tactile skin with adaptive AI, the project aims to let robots sense and remember contact, recognise risks such as a slipping object, and correct their movements, or stop when uncertainty is too high. It is funded by Independent Research Fund Denmark and begins on 1 March 2027 as a collaboration between Aarhus University and the University of Southern Denmark.

Problem and ambition

Why touch, and what STORM will do

Why touch

Robotic foundation models can already see, read and act. Yet much of what matters in manipulation is felt rather than seen. An object begins to slip, or a grasp lands differently from how it was planned, and a camera may show nothing unusual until the task has already failed. Touch is the sense that reveals when the physical world has stopped matching the robot’s expectations.

STORM starts from this observation and asks how a robot can use touch to detect unexpected physical changes and adjust its actions safely, so that manipulation becomes more reliable under changing real-world conditions.

What STORM will do

The project will combine soft, fluid-filled tactile skin with adaptive AI that lets a robot sense contact and remember it. Every kind of touch leaves its own signature in the signals of the soft skin, and a memory of such signatures will tell the robot what contact to expect as it acts. When the contact it feels departs from what it expects, the robot will be able to recognise risks such as a slipping object and correct its movements, or stop when uncertainty is too high to continue safely.

STORM will study these stages of sensing, memory, risk assessment and correction together, with the goal of safer and more reliable robotic manipulation in changing real-world conditions.

Team

Leadership and collaboration

STORM is led from Aarhus University by Behzad Bozorgtabar, Principal Investigator, who heads the Adaptive & Agentic AI (A3) Lab in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. The project co-PI is Professor Ahmad Rafsanjani of the University of Southern Denmark (SDU), and the project’s partners are SDU and SDU Soft Robotics.

The project is a collaboration between Aarhus University and the University of Southern Denmark, with the grant of DKK 7.2 million shared across the two universities. It will begin on 1 March 2027.
